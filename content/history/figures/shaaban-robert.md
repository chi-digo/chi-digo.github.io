# Shaaban Robert: The Poet Born to a Digo Mother at Machui

## Overview

**Era:** 1909–1962 (one study gives 1902 for his birth)
**Role:** Swahili poet, novelist and essayist; colonial civil servant; member of Tanga Town Council
**Region:** Machui, south of Tanga, present-day Tanzania

---

Shaaban Robert is the best-known Swahili writer of the twentieth century. People call him "Mr. Swahili", the father of Swahili literature and the poet laureate of Swahili. He was born at Machui, in the countryside south of Tanga, to a Digo mother, Mwanamwema binti Mwidau. His father was Yao. Shaaban himself did not call himself Digo or Yao. He was one of the first people to call himself simply a Swahili.

He was not a Digo writer, then. But his mother's people were Digo, he grew up among them, and he lies buried in Digo country. That is why his story has a place here.

## A Child of Machui

Most sources say he was born on 1 January 1909 at Vibamba (Vibambani), a part of Machui village. A study by A. Taib, published by the University of Dar es Salaam in 2009, gives the year as 1902. His father was named Ufukwe, Selemani and Robert, and as a boy Shaaban was known as Shaaban bin Selemani. The name "Robert" came from his father.

Taib wrote that Shaaban's character and gifts came from two peoples meeting in one family: on his father's side Yao from Ruvuma, and on his mother's side Digo from Tanga. A student essay published online in 2016 adds that his mother came from the lineage of Mwalimu Chihere, that she too called herself Swahili, and that Shaaban was the fourth of her fourteen children. The same essay says his father died in September 1918, and that the boy was then sent to Dar es Salaam to live with a grandfather.

## School and Service

Shaaban studied in Dar es Salaam from 1922 to 1926. He left school with a School Leaving Certificate, second in a class of eleven. That was the end of his formal education.

He spent the rest of his working life in the colonial civil service. He was a customs officer at several posts from 1926 to 1944, worked in the Game Department from 1944 to 1946, in the Tanga District Office from 1946 to 1952, and in the Survey Office from 1952 to 1960. He also sat on the Tanga Town Council. He served on bodies that looked after the Swahili language, including the East African Swahili Committee and the East African Literature Bureau. The British government made him a Member of the Order of the British Empire (MBE).

## The Writer

Shaaban wrote poems, novels, essays and a life story, all in Swahili. Many readers start with *Kusadikika* (1951), a story about an imaginary country. It opens:

> "Kusadikika is a country whose existence can only be imagined in the mind."

His other books include the novels *Kufikirika* and *Adili na Nduguze* (1952), the poetry collection *Pambo la Lugha*, a life of the singer Siti binti Saad, and *Utubora Mkulima*. His autobiography, *Maisha Yangu na Baada ya Miaka Hamsini*, won the Margaret Wrong Memorial Prize for African literature. *Utenzi wa Vita vya Uhuru*, a long poem of about 3,000 stanzas on the Second World War, came out after his death. His writing helped Swahili to be adopted as a national language in Tanzania and Kenya.

## Why He Belongs in Digo History

Among the Digo, the most important family tie runs through the mother. A Digo belongs to a *fuko*, the clan of the mother's side. By that reckoning, the child of a Digo mother belongs to her *fuko*, whoever the father's people are.

Shaaban also grew up in the Digo countryside south of Tanga, and his family at Machui shaped his imagination. A study published in 2021 by the University of Dar es Salaam journal *Mulika* says that his family at Machui knew the arts of healing and divination. It names his mother's first husband, Kibwana Jafari, and his elder half-brother, Jafari Kibwana, as men remembered for changing their shape at will. The study argues that this world lies behind the spirits and shape-changing in *Adili na Nduguze* and *Kufikirika*.

The evidence for his Digo mother needs to be stated honestly. It rests mainly on one chain of scholarship: Taib's study of 2009, as cited in the 2021 *Mulika* article. Wikipedia says the same without a source. We have found nothing that contradicts it.

## Death and Memory

Shaaban Robert died in June 1962. Most sources give the date as 22 June. Others say he died on 20 June and was buried on 22 June; they disagree on whether he died in Tanga or in Dar es Salaam. All agree that he was buried at Machui, in the family graveyard at Vibambani.

Schools and streets carry his name, including Shaaban Robert Secondary School in Dar es Salaam, founded around 1963, and a primary school. There is a hall in his name at the Tanga Regional Library and a Shaaban Robert Festival. At Machui itself there is a modest memorial house and a library building that has stood unfinished for more than ten years. A Swahili essay of 2025 says his grave lies in the bush, with little to mark it. In 2026 his relatives and Swahili scholars said the site does not reflect what he gave the nation. For the Digo of Tanga, the greatest Swahili writer lies among them, in his mother's country.
